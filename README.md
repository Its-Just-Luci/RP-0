Welcome to RP-1 SSTs, a pair of programs to develop supersonic transports:
</p>

The programs will be split into Large and Medium scale SSTs. While in reality, we only ever recieved the larger-scale Concorde and TU-144, smaller scale SSTs with shorter range and smaller passenger requirements were proposed.

A few small-scale SST proposals include: 

Convair's Model 58-9, a Mach 2.4 transport with a 4,600 km range and seating for 52.
Sud Aviation's Super Caravelle, a Mach 2 transport with a 3,000km range and seating for 70.
Boom's Overture proposal, a Mach 1.7 transport with an 8,000km range, and seating for up to 80.

Some larger-scale proposals include:
Boeing's 2707, a Mach 3 SST with a 4,000km range and seating for over 200.
Lockheed's L-2000, a Mach 2.7 transport with a 7,400km range and seating for 230.
North American's NAC-60, a Mach 2.6 transport with a range of 6,300km and a passenger capacity of 187.

Largely, the two programs will be split into 100 or more passengers being considered 'large' and anything from 50-100 being considered 'medium'. Medium SSTs will be faster, offer less funding, but also be less risky. Large scale will be based around the development periods we got, ranging from 10-20 years. Both programs will include new science experiments that require large, long-range, high-altitude supersonic aircraft. Things like emissions testing, takeoff noise and sonic-boom analysis and supersonic continous eclipse imaging will be one this list.

Subscale test programs will be required, with some of these being optionally completable during the _Advanced X-Planes_ program. These will be based around a large variety of real-world aircraft and test programs, from the B-58 and B-70, to the Fairey Delta II, and NASA's [National Sonic Boom Test Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_City_sonic_boom_tests) This will add variety to a rather one-note program, while rewarding players who completed Advanced X-Planes with the ability to jump straight in to full-scale testing (as was the case with the Europeans)

These programs will, however, be limited to the most basic of SSTs. Things like NASA's High Speed Civil Transport program, or the many proposed Mach 4+ transports, will be included down the road in a potential advanced civil transport program.
